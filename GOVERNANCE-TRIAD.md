# Governance Foundation

Date: 2026-05-15
Status: canonical framing

## The Four Governance Questions

- **WHAT is there?** -> `AI-SBOM`
- **HOW did it get there?** -> `CBOM`
- **WHO is acting?** -> `AINS / .aint`
- **WHY do we believe this?** -> `JIS`

## The Architectural Truth

`JIS` is not merely a fourth neighbor next to the other three.

It sits **under** them as the trust foundation:

- `AI-SBOM` states what is present
- `CBOM` states how it arrived
- `AINS / .aint` states who is acting
- `JIS` states why those claims are believable

Without `JIS`, the layers above are only assertions.

With `JIS`, they become attested claims.

## Why This Matters

A model is statically present.

An actor is actively operating.

That is the key distinction:

- a model represents capability
- an actor represents identity plus action

Without the `what`, you do not know what artifacts, models, datasets,
or software are present.

Without the `how`, you do not know provenance, custody, continuity, or
legitimacy.

Without the `who`, you do not know which active identity is invoking,
routing, deciding, or continuing.

Without the `why`, you do not know whether any of those claims are
cryptographically anchored, hardware-bound, or authoritative.

## The Governance Claim

Together these four answers form governance:

- inventory truth
- provenance truth
- active identity truth
- trust foundation

Or shorter:

- `AI-SBOM` says what is present.
- `CBOM` says how it arrived.
- `AINS` says who is acting.
- `JIS` says why we believe it.

That is why the stack matters.

## Structural Reading

The stack can be read as:

- `WHAT` -> inventory
- `HOW` -> causality
- `WHO` -> active identity
- `WHY` -> cryptographic authority

And structurally:

- `AI-SBOM`, `CBOM`, and `AINS` answer the visible governance questions
- `JIS` underwrites all three
